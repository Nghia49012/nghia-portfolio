## Post 2: Debunking the Gmail Open Rate Myth

**Core Insight:** Don’t chase viral deliverability explanations—triangulate metrics (opens *and* clicks) across time; if clicks stay steady while opens fall, it suggests measurement/engagement recording changes, not “inflated opens,” and the inbox will reward real behavior—not panic.

There's a lot of noise right now about Gmail open rates being inflated by image prefetching. That behavior isn’t new. Gmail has been doing some version of this for years.

That’s worth calling out for folks who aren’t aware, but when I see people treating this like a brand new explanation for what’s happening right now… that’s where things start to fall apart (including my patience).

What’s changing right now doesn’t look like “more of the same”… it looks like something else shifting in how engagement is being recorded.

Across ESPs and senders, I’m seeing Gmail opens trending slightly down since late February / early March… while clicks are holding steady.

If this were just more prefetching, you’d expect opens to go up… or at least stay flat.

A downward trend with stable clicks points to fewer automatic opens being triggered, not more.

So yes, something does seem to be shifting at Gmail. Just not in the direction being claimed.

---

This is a good example of where deliverability advice starts to fall apart. It needs more context… not years-old behavior reframed as something new and urgent.

A metric moves. Someone notices. A post goes up with arrows and urgency. And suddenly practitioners are optimizing for the wrong thing… or panicking about a problem that doesn’t match what’s actually happening in production.

The inbox doesn’t care about the post. It responds to behavior.

## Post 3: The 3 Stages of Fixing Deliverability Issues

**Core Insight:** Deliverability fixes fail most often in **execution** and especially **follow-up**—the playbook should assign ownership, require proof-of-fix, and reduce “alert fatigue” so teams focus on resolving root causes instead of closing tickets.

Sharing this because it feels like an important shift in how we think about deliverability.

There are really three parts to the puzzle:

1. Detection
2. Execution (the fix)
3. Follow-up

Detection:

This has honestly been “solved” for a while now… to the point where we’ve all got alerts coming out of our ears. (And someone on the team is stuck babysitting them. 😒)

Execution:

Still very much a human problem.

Because it’s rarely just a technical fix… it’s explaining what’s wrong, why it matters, and convincing someone to prioritize fixing it.

Follow-up:

This is where things really tend to fall apart.

Tickets get messy fast, ownership gets fuzzy, and things get marked “done” without anyone really confirming if the issue is… actually fixed. 🤨

That’s the part I find interesting here.

Translated out of AI-speak, Bram is basically saying:

“We flag issues and keep track of what’s still broken vs. what’s fixed… with proof… so your team can stop chasing email ghosts (and stop looking over your shoulder).”

Tools like this don’t replace execution… and you should watch out for tools that claim to. But they do cut down the noise so you can focus on fixing what matters instead of just managing alerts and closing tickets.

I got a demo of what Bram’s been building with Engagor.AI… this is just one piece of it, but it stood out.
