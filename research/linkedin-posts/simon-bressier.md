## Post 1: DNSBL Monitoring (The “Silent Failure”)

**Core Insight:** Blocklist listings are often silent and immediate—monitoring DNSBLs proactively (with timestamps, severity weighting, and history) prevents “we found out from a customer” incidents and turns deliverability from reactive firefighting into an operational system.

The worst scenario in deliverability is not to end up in spam, it's not to know that you are there. We have built a DNSBL monitoring system that checks 47 blocklists for you every hour, so that you never have to learn it from a customer.

Blocklists (DNSBL) are public lists of IPs and domains identified as sources of spam. The receiving servers consult them in real time during each SMTP connection to accept or reject an email.

Which makes it treacherous: no one warns you when you appear. Not your ESP, not Gmail, not Outlook. Your emails silently start to bounce, and you find out when a customer calls you to say they're not receiving anything anymore.

This is how the monitoring we have set up in Sender Audit works.

We query 47 DNSBLs in parallel at each cycle:

- 21 IP lists: Spamhaus ZEN, SpamCop, Barracuda, SORBS, Abusix, blocklist.de, 0spam, DroneBL, UCEPROTECT, etc.
- 26 domain lists: Spamhaus DBL, URIBL, SURBL, NordSpam, SORBS RHSBL, abuse.ch, etc.

The checks run in parallel and are completed in a few seconds. The technical principle is simple: a resolution to 127.0.0.x = listed, NXDOMAIN = clean. Binary, without grey area.

When a listing is detected, the provider, its level of criticality (Spamhaus weighs infinitely more than a small regional list), and the exact timestamp are recorded. The delistings are also traced - the history remains accessible. A Spamhaus listing triggers a degraded mode that directly impacts the health score of the domain or IP.

For an agency that manages several clients, this is the difference between being proactive and managing crises on a chain.

## Post 2: Visual Attention Heatmaps (Objective Creative QA)

**Core Insight:** Email “design effectiveness” can be measured—using salience heatmaps plus an impact score (CTA focus, whitespace, readability, above-the-fold, text size, contrast) gives a repeatable QA checklist that improves scanability and conversion across desktop/mobile.

You know what your email says. But do you know what your recipients look at first?

Look at this image. It's an attention heatmap generated by Sender Audit on a real email.

Red areas = strong visual attention.  
Blue zones = ignored.

This analysis uses a visual salience model that simulates the behavior of the human gaze on your email, in desktop AND mobile versions.

What it reveals, in a few seconds:

- Is your AHU in a hot or cold zone?
- Is your key content visible without scrolling?
- Is your email too dense or airy?

Sender Audit calculates an Impact Score (0 to 100) based on 6 criteria:

- CTA focus - is the eye drawn to the action?
- Aeration – ratio of white space to dense content
- Readability - ease of reading (Flesch score)
- Above the fold - a CTA visible without scrolling?
- Text size - large enough to read on mobile?
- Contrast - WCAG AA compliant?

The result: an objective visual diagnosis, not a feeling.

Did you think your email was visually effective? Test it out. You might be surprised.

## Post 3: Gmail 102 KB Clipping (Hidden Conversion + Compliance Risk)

**Core Insight:** Gmail clips HTML emails over **102 KB**, which can remove CTAs, unsubscribe links, and tracking—keep templates lightweight (avoid base64 images, reduce verbose inline CSS, split long content) to protect conversions, compliance, and measurement accuracy.

Gmail cuts your email beyond 102 KB. Not 102 MB. 102 KB. How much is your last newsletter?

Gmail clipping is one of the most underrated problems in email marketing.

When your HTML exceeds 102KB, Gmail truncates the message and displays a "View full message" link. Most people don't click.

Consequences:

- Your CTA at the bottom of the email disappears
- Your unsubscribe link too (hello compliance issues)
- Your tracking pixel is cut off → your stats are skewed

Common causes:

- Images encoded in base64 in HTML (instead of hosted)
- Inlineated CSS too wordy (copy-paste from a builder)
- Content that is too long for a single shipment

Sender Audit measures the exact weight of your email, identifies oversized images, and warns you if Gmail is going to clip.
